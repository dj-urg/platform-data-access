Chapter 2 · The EU gateway

DSA Article 40

The legal backbone of researcher data access in Europe — and the single most important decision in this guide: the 40(12) public-data route versus the 40(4) vetted-researcher route.

What Article 40 gives you

Regulation (EU) 2022/2065 (the DSA) designates services with more than 45 million EU users as Very Large Online Platforms (VLOPs) or Very Large Online Search Engines (VLOSEs) — including Facebook, Instagram, TikTok, YouTube, X, Snapchat, Pinterest, LinkedIn, Wikipedia, Google Search and Bing (full list: ec.europa.eu — designated VLOPs and VLOSEs). Article 40 obliges them to provide researchers with data for research on systemic risks in the EU (Art. 34: disinformation, electoral integrity, protection of minors, mental health, illegal content, fundamental rights) and on the platforms' risk-mitigation measures (Art. 35). Telegram is not designated, so no DSA route exists for it; Reddit was designated on 31 August 2026, with its Art. 40 obligations applying from around the end of 2026 (the same round designated ChatGPT as the first AI-assistant VLOSE — see Alt-tech & beyond).

There are two distinct routes, and choosing the right one is the single most important decision in this guide:

  • Article 40(12) — publicly accessible data, no vetting. You apply directly to the platform. This is the legal basis of the Meta Content Library, the TikTok Research API, the Google Researcher Program and X's DSA researcher form. Conditions: independence from commercial interests, disclosure of funding, and a research purpose connected to EU systemic risks; unlike the 40(4) route, formal affiliation with a research organisation is not required (researchers at not-for-profit bodies qualify). Typical turnaround: weeks.
  • Article 40(4) — non-public (internal) data, vetted researchers. For data no public tool exposes (e.g., internal metrics, moderation data). You must be vetted by a Digital Services Coordinator (DSC) through the European Commission's DSA Data Access Portal. Heavier procedure; up to 80 working days plus negotiation. Operational since 29 October 2025 under Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2025/2050.

Applying as a VUB researcher — Art. 40(4), non-public data

Belgium's Digital Services Coordinator is BIPT (bipt.be — vetted researchers, contact: dsa@bipt.be). Because no VLOP is established in Belgium, BIPT performs an initial assessment and forwards your file to the DSC of the country where the platform is established — for most platforms Ireland (Coimisiún na Meán), which takes the final decision.

  1. Check that your target platform is a designated VLOP/VLOSE, and that your question genuinely requires non-public data. If public data suffices, use the platform's Art. 40(12) tool instead — much faster.
  2. Register on the DSA Data Access Portal. One principal researcher leads the application; name all participating researchers in it.
  3. Prepare the application: proof of affiliation with a research organisation; a signed declaration of independence from commercial interests; disclosure of all funding (pending funding may be disclosed as such); a data security and GDPR compliance plan (consult VUB's DPO, dpo@vub.be, early); a precise description of the data requested (fields, formats, time periods) with a necessity and proportionality justification tied to systemic risks; and a commitment to publish results openly and free of charge.
  4. Submit via the portal, selecting BIPT as your DSC, or apply directly to the DSC of establishment. Write in English — the file travels abroad.
  5. The deciding DSC assesses vetting and, if granted, sends a reasoned request to the platform. The platform has 15 days to request amendments on limited grounds (it lacks the data; significant security vulnerabilities; trade secrets) and may initiate mediation. The deciding DSC must act on the application within 80 working days (extendable in justified cases); the platform's amendment window and any mediation can add further time.

Realism check

The procedure is new and early practice is slow: by early 2026 the Irish DSC had published guidance but issued no reasoned request yet, and at least one application had been rejected; as of October 2026 no reasoned request had been publicly reported. Criticisms documented by researchers and civil society include the platform-friendly mediation design, reliance on platform-run secure environments, and vague trade-secret exceptions. Plan Art. 40(4) access on a horizon of many months, and keep a public-data fallback. Useful ongoing trackers: the DSA Observatory and the Weizenbaum Institute's data access publications (Seiling et al., 2025).
