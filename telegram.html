Chapter 8 · Telegram

Telegram

No research program, no DSA route — research runs on the full Telegram API with your own account, self-service from day one.

What
No research program and no DSA route (not a designated VLOP). Research runs on the full Telegram API (MTProto, Telegram's native protocol) with your own user account — the same API official apps use.
Who
Anyone with a Telegram account; no application or review.
Cost
Free.
Timeline
Credentials in ~10 minutes, self-service.

Getting API credentials

  1. Create a Telegram account with an official app. Many researchers use a dedicated research account/SIM to separate research from personal use; note that fresh accounts doing immediate bulk collection face elevated ban risk, and each phone number can hold only one API registration.
  2. Log in at my.telegram.org with that phone number (the login code arrives inside Telegram).
  3. Open “API development tools”, fill in the short application form (app title, short name, platform), and receive your api_id and api_hash. Keep both secret — together with your login they grant full account access.
  4. Use them from Python with Telethon (actively maintained — development now lives on Codeberg — and the academic standard) — or let 4CAT handle collection without coding (see the toolkits chapter): its Telegram data source takes your api_id/api_hash and logs in as you. The original Pyrogram is unmaintained (its fork Kurigram is active), and check the maintenance status of OSINT wrappers such as Telepathy-Community before relying on them.

What researchers collect, and the rules

A logged-in account can read the complete history of any public channel or open group it can see as a normal user: posts, views and forward counts, reactions, comments, and the forwarding metadata used to snowball-sample from seed channels — the standard discovery method; see the TeleScope dataset (Gangopadhyay et al., 2025) for a reusable large-scale example built this way.

  • Rate limits are undocumented: the server answers over-eager clients with FLOOD_WAIT errors. Username/link resolution is the most heavily limited call; resolve channels by ID where possible, add delays, and let Telethon's built-in flood handling work. Never parallelise across multiple “farmed” accounts.
  • Ban risk is real: Telegram warns on its API-credentials page that accounts using the API for flooding, spamming or abuse “will be banned forever”, and that all unofficial-client logins are “put under observation”. Wrongful bans can be appealed with an explanation of the research at recover@telegram.org.
  • Terms of service: Telegram's API terms (§1.5) prohibit using platform data to train AI/ML models — relevant if your pipeline involves fine-tuning. Scraping public channels is otherwise not addressed and is established research practice; private/invite-only groups are another matter entirely (covert joining raises consent problems most ethics boards treat as human-subjects research).
  • GDPR: messages and usernames are personal data. Rely on the research provisions (Art. 89 safeguards, Art. 14(5)(b)), do a DPIA for large collections, avoid harvesting member lists, and de-identify users in publications. Consult VUB's DPO (dpo@vub.be).
